# Code of Conduct

This is a small project maintained by one person. The rules below are short
because they are meant to be read, and they are here so that nobody has to
guess what happens if something goes wrong.

## What is expected

Discuss the work. Assume the other person is trying to be useful, including
when they are wrong. Say what you mean plainly — bluntness is fine, and a
review that says "this breaks X" is not rudeness.

Not everyone here shares a first language, a time zone, or a level of
experience. A question that looks obvious to you was not obvious to whoever
asked it.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, including unwanted attention after being asked to
  stop.
- Attacking a person instead of their argument — their competence, their
  background, their identity, their English.
- Publishing anything private about somebody: an address, an employer, a
  message sent to you in confidence.
- Sexual attention or imagery. This is a URL shortener; there is no context
  in which it belongs here.
- Deliberate disruption: derailing threads, reopening a settled decision to
  wear people down, filing issues to make a point rather than to fix
  something.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the project has a surface: issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, code comments, and any conversation where you are speaking
as a participant in this project.

## Reporting

Write to **maizekeen@internet.ru**. It reaches the maintainer and nobody
else.

> [!NOTE]
> That address is for conduct. A **security** problem goes through GitHub's
> private reporting instead — see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md). Please do not
> open a public issue for either one.

Include what happened and where, with links if there are any. You do not
have to be certain, and you do not have to be the person it happened to.

What to expect:

| | |
|---|---|
| **First reply** | Within 10 days, saying what I understood and what I intend to do |
| **Confidentiality** | Your name stays between us unless you say otherwise, and it is never given to the person reported |
| **If I go quiet** | Ten days without an answer means the message fell through the cracks, not that it was dismissed. Send it again |

That last row is here for the same reason it is in `SECURITY.md`: a project
run by one person can go quiet for ordinary human reasons, and nobody
should have to guess whether they are being ignored.

## What happens then

Whatever fits. Roughly, in order:

1. **A word in private.** Most of what lands here is a bad day rather than
   a pattern, and this is where it ends.
2. **A public correction**, when the exchange happened in public and left
   something standing that should not stand.
3. **A pause** — being asked to stay out of a thread, or off the tracker,
   for a while.
4. **A block**, for a pattern that continues after the above, or for a
   single act serious enough not to need a second.

I will say which of these I am doing and why. If you think I got it wrong,
say so — I would rather hear it than have you leave quietly. One person
deciding is a real limitation of a project this size, and the honest answer
is that there is no appeal beyond a second conversation with the same
person.

## Applies to me too

The maintainer is not exempt. If the problem is my behaviour, the address
above is still the place to say so, and the alternative — saying it in a
public issue — is one I will not hold against you.
